When guests choose accommodation on Booking or Airbnb, the first thing they see is the photos. A good photo shows a room at its best: a made bed, sunlight at the window, a lovely terrace. But after twenty photos like that, guests still don't know the most important thing — what it feels like to be in that house.

Where is the bathroom in relation to the bedroom? How big is the garden, really? What do you see when you step out onto the terrace? Photos are great for details and weak at conveying space. That's exactly where an FPV flythrough does what a photo can't.

## Photos show rooms, a flythrough shows the house

An FPV drone is a small drone with guards around the propellers, built for flying through enclosed spaces. The pilot flies it “first person” — through goggles they see exactly what the camera sees — so it can pass precisely through doors, hallways and stairwells.

The result is one unbroken shot. The camera starts in the garden, enters through the front door, moves through the living room and kitchen, climbs up to the bedrooms and comes out onto the terrace. No cuts and no jumping from one place to another. Viewers don't look at a series of pictures; they “walk” through the house in the order they'd explore it themselves.

That's why a flythrough gives a sense of layout and size that even the best gallery can't convey. After half a minute of footage, guests know where everything is and can picture themselves in that space — and that's usually the moment the booking decision is made.

For rural tourism this matters even more. Guests don't come just for the room, but for the yard, the peace and the nature around the house. A flythrough that connects the inside and the outside shows exactly why people choose a place like this.

## How the house is prepared for a flythrough

An FPV flythrough doesn't forgive clutter. Because everything is seen in a single shot, there's nowhere to hide behind the edge of the frame. So before filming we walk through the property with you and agree on a few things:

- every room should be tidy, including the ones that don't seem important, because the drone flies through them;
- interior doors are open and the flight path is clear;
- the lights are on and the curtains are open;
- distracting little things — chargers, bags, papers — are moved out of shot;
- nobody is in the house, unless we agree otherwise.

We plan the flight path in advance, following the route a guest would take into the house. We usually film several passes and pick the one that flows best in the edit.

## You still need photos

An FPV flythrough doesn't replace photos; it complements them. A listing still starts with a cover photo, and guests still want to zoom in on the bathroom or the view from the terrace. The best results come from a combination: photos for the details, a classic drone flight for the location and surroundings, and an FPV flythrough for the sense of space.
