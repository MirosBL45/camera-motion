When people get in touch about drone filming, they often don't know that there are two very different kinds of aerial footage. Both are called “drone filming”, but they create a completely different impression. One is calm and wide, the other fast and close. Here's the difference, and when each one makes sense.

## Classic drone: calm and breadth

A classic drone is what most people picture when they hear the word. The aircraft hovers steadily in the air, and a camera on a gimbal delivers a calm, clean shot. We use a DJI Mini 3 Pro for this.

The strength of a classic drone is the overview. It shows where something is and what surrounds it: a church in the middle of town, a house with a garden and the path to the beach, a restaurant on the riverbank. It can hover in one spot, rise slowly to reveal a space, or follow a building from a long way off.

Shots like these are slow and elegant. They open a film well, place the viewer in the setting and give a breather between dynamic scenes.

## FPV drone: movement and speed

An FPV drone does something entirely different. The pilot wears goggles and sees exactly what the camera sees, as if sitting inside the drone. That's why it can be flown precisely through tight spaces — through doors and hallways, between trees and pillars. For FPV we fly a DJI Avata 2, which is built for exactly that and has guards around the propellers.

The result is a single unbroken flight that makes viewers feel as if they're flying themselves. FPV brings movement, speed and closeness, and captures shots a classic drone simply can't: passing through an open door, flying down a hallway and out onto a terrace, dropping from the roof straight into the garden.

The Avata 2 can also fly classically, outdoors, when needed, but its real strength lies in spaces other drones can't enter.

## Where each one makes sense

**Weddings.** A classic drone is almost a must here: the church, the venue and the landscape from the air give the film breadth. FPV is the effect people remember — a flight through the empty hall before the programme begins, or an opening shot that draws the viewer into the day. **Real estate.** A classic drone shows the location, the surroundings and the access — everything buyers or guests want to know before they step inside. FPV shows the interior and the layout, in one shot, like a walk through the space. We've written about why that flythrough works so well in [Why an FPV flythrough sells a property better than photos](blog:fpv-prolet-nekretnine).

**Businesses.** A restaurant, hotel, gym or shop can make use of both. A classic drone shows where you are and what the area looks like, while an FPV flight from the entrance to the last corner shows straight away what it's like at your place. A short FPV shot at the start of a spot is often what holds attention in the first few seconds.

## A combination gives the most

If you have to choose just one, choose based on what matters more to show: where something is, or what it feels like to be inside. But the best videos usually use both. The classic drone sets the scene, FPV takes the viewer into it, and the camera on the ground captures the details and the people.
