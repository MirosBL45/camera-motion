A wedding video is one of the few things that can't be done twice. You can order another cake and book a different band, but the day itself is filmed only once. That's why choosing a videographer matters more than it seems at first — and why the decision shouldn't rest on one beautiful clip on Instagram.

A beautiful clip shows how a videographer works when everything goes to plan. The questions below show how they work when it doesn't. Ask them at your first meeting, without hesitation. A good videographer will answer straight away and in specifics.

## 1. What equipment do you use?

You don't need to know what the numbers on a lens mean. It's enough to hear the answer to three things: what happens when it gets dark, how movement through the venue looks, and whether there will be aerial shots.

Most weddings end late in the evening, in a hall with dimmed lights. A camera and lenses that handle low light keep faces sharp and clean even at midnight. A gimbal keeps the shot steady while the videographer moves between tables. If a drone is part of the offer, ask who flies it — ours is flown by a pilot registered with the Civil Aviation Directorate, in line with regulations.

## 2. What if something goes wrong?

This is the question people ask least, and it tells you the most. A battery runs flat, a card fills up, rain changes the schedule. None of that is unusual on a day that lasts twelve hours.

Ask whether the videographer carries spare batteries and cards, what they do if a camera fails in the middle of the ceremony, and how they keep the footage safe after the shoot, before editing begins. The answer doesn't have to be long, but there has to be one. If a videographer hasn't thought about it, that tells you enough.

## 3. When does the finished video arrive, and in what form?

After the wedding everyone wants to see the video as soon as possible, and editing a whole day takes time. There's nothing wrong with a delivery time of a few weeks — the only problem is when it isn't agreed.

Ask how long you'll wait, and make sure that deadline is written into the agreement. Ask how the video arrives, too. We deliver it digitally, as a download link in full quality, together with versions ready for social media. One piece of advice that applies to any videographer: as soon as it arrives, copy the video to an external drive, because download links don't last forever.

## 4. What exactly does the price include?

Two quotes for the same amount can mean completely different things. One covers a single videographer until the first dance, the other two videographers until the end of the night, a drone and a highlight spot.

So ask what exactly you get: how many hours of filming, how many people are filming, whether a drone and a vertical version for social media are included, whether you get both the full edited film of the day and a short spot, and whether a venue outside the city costs extra. ## 5. How do you record sound?

The picture is what you remember, but the difference between a good wedding video and a great one is usually the sound. Vows spoken quietly, the best man's toast, the song you walked into the hall to — if they aren't clear, the video loses half its value.

Ask where the microphones go. We put wireless microphones on the vows, speeches and toasts, take a feed from the band's mixing desk, and use ambient microphones to capture the atmosphere of the room. Everything is then mixed in the edit. The only thing we ask of you is to let the band know in advance that we'll be plugging into their desk.

## Finally: how do you feel after the conversation?

Beyond all the answers, pay attention to how you feel after that first conversation. Your videographer is beside you all day, in your most personal moments. You should feel comfortable with them whether the camera is rolling or not.
