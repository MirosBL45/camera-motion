A short video is the fastest way for people to get to know your business today. Before they visit your restaurant, hotel or gym for the first time, most of them will look you up online — and a video of a minute or two tells them more than a long description.

But many business owners aren't sure where to start. What do they need to know before filming? How long does it take? What do they need to prepare? Here's the path from idea to finished film when we work together.

## 1. A conversation about the goal

It all starts with a conversation — not about cameras, but about you. We want to know what you'd like to achieve: for new people to get to know you, to present a new space, to announce a service, or simply to have a great video on your website.

Where the video will be published matters just as much. A video for your homepage, for Instagram and for a paid ad don't look the same: they differ in length, pace and format. When we know that in advance, we plan the whole shoot so you get every version you need from it.

## 2. The idea and the shot list

Once the goal is clear, we write a short outline and a shot list. It isn't a long document, but a list of what we want to show and the order in which the story flows: the space, the people, the product or service, the atmosphere.

At this stage we also choose the filming date together. For a restaurant, that may be when the kitchen is in full swing; for a hotel, a morning with the best light; for a gym, a time when the floor is busy. Planning ahead means we don't waste time on the day, and you know exactly what we'll be doing.

## 3. Filming

On the day, we arrive with the equipment the plan calls for. A camera on a gimbal films the interior, the details and people at work. A drone shows the location and the surroundings from the air. Where it makes sense, we add an FPV flight — a single unbroken shot through the doors and the space that holds attention from the very first seconds.

We do our best to disrupt your work as little as possible. The shoot is planned so your staff and guests can carry on as normal, while we capture natural moments instead of staged ones.

Filming on location can bring unexpected challenges, too. One of our shoots for a boat rental company on the Adriatic was a lot more eventful than we had planned — we wrote about it in [A yacht, the open sea and a drone that didn't come back](blog:snimanje-jahte-dronom-na-moru).

## 4. Editing

From the footage, we edit a spot in your style, with your logo, colours and music that suits your brand.

## 5. Delivery in the right formats

You receive the finished video digitally, in all the formats we agreed on. Most often there are two:

- **horizontal 16:9**, for your website and YouTube;
- **vertical 9:16**, for Instagram Reels and Stories.

Both versions come from the same shoot, but they aren't the same — the vertical cut has its own pace and shots adapted for the phone. One round of revisions is included in the price, so you can ask for changes before you publish the video.

## What a business should prepare

The best promo videos happen when the business is ready for filming too. Before the shoot, it's worth preparing:

- **the location** — a tidy space, the lights on, and anything you don't want in shot put away;
- **the people** — which members of staff will appear on camera, and whether they're happy to; people naturally at work always look better than posing;
- **what you're showing** — products, dishes, rooms or a service, ready at the moment we film them.

If you're not sure what you need, we sort that out in the first conversation.
