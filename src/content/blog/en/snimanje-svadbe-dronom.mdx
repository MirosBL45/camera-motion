An aerial shot has become an almost essential part of a wedding video. A church revealed above the treetops, a restaurant on the riverbank, the road the couple arrives along — these are images you simply can't capture from the ground. But drone filming is more than getting an aircraft into the air. Behind a few seconds of beautiful footage there is preparation that starts long before the day itself.

Here's how it works with us, and what's worth knowing if you're thinking about a drone at your wedding.

## Preparation starts weeks ahead

The first thing we want to know is the schedule of the day: where you're getting ready, where the church or registry office is, where the reception is, and whether you're planning a walk or a photo session somewhere outdoors. Each of those locations is a separate story for the drone.

Locations we don't know, we visit in advance or go through with you using photos and maps. We look at where the drone can take off safely, where the trees, poles and wires are, which direction the light will come from when you're there, and which shot makes sense at each spot. That way we don't waste time searching on the day — we know exactly where to stand and what to film.

## Venue permissions

For every flight we check the flight zones and fly in line with regulations — the pilot is registered with the Civil Aviation Directorate. That's our part of the job, and you don't need to worry about it.

What you should pay attention to are the rules of the venue itself. Some churches, restaurants and private estates have nothing against a drone, some ask for the flight to be announced, and a few don't allow it at all. The simplest thing is to mention, when you book the hall or the church, that there will be drone filming. If you're not sure whom to ask, give us the contact person at the venue and we'll check together.

## Weather

A drone doesn't fly in rain or strong wind. That isn't the videographer being overcautious, it's about safety — yours, your guests' and the aircraft's. That's why we follow the forecast for days before the wedding and agree in advance what we'll do if the weather doesn't allow a flight.

If you'd like to know what's realistic to film when your wedding falls in winter or the forecast turns, we've written about it in more detail in [A winter or rainy wedding](blog:vencanje-zimi-ili-po-kisi).

## How the drone fits in with the camera on the ground

A drone doesn't replace classic filming — it complements it. The emotion of the day is carried by the camera on the ground: faces, tears, hugs, the words you can hear. The drone adds space and atmosphere: where everything is happening, and how it looks from a perspective none of the guests have.

In practice, this means the drone flies in short, pre-planned windows. We film the church and the venue from the air before the programme begins, the couple's arrival, the surroundings and the landscape, and the two of you on a walk, when there are a few quiet minutes for it. The drone never flies over the guests — shots with people are taken from the side and from a safe distance, and during the ceremony, the vows and the speeches only the camera on the ground is working. The guests barely notice it.

In the edit, the aerial and ground footage come together into one story. It's exactly that combination that makes a video feel like a film rather than just a record of the event.

## What you can do

A few small things make the job easier and the result better:

- send us the schedule of the day as soon as you have it, with the addresses of all locations;
- ask the venue and the church whether they have rules about drone filming;
- leave a little time in the schedule just for the two of you, ideally before sunset, when the light is at its best;
- don't worry about the weather — we agree on a plan B in advance.
